# Fivestars (US local-merchant loyalty network) and SumUp — network model and marketing monetization

Research date: 2026-09-29. Historical facts are dated where the source gives a date. web.archive.org could not be fetched from this environment, so archived Fivestars pricing pages were NOT consulted; historical pricing below rests on third-party aggregators and merchant reviews (lower confidence).

## 1. Network model: one consumer account across merchants, discovery, cross-merchant promotion, new-customer data

### Takeaway
Fivestars was a genuine shared-consumer network: one consumer identity (phone number) and one app across all merchants, with network discovery built into three touchpoints (the in-store touchscreen at OTHER non-competing merchants, the post-visit SMS recap, and the app/website map). The "new customers from the network" product was sold as "Fivestars Acquisition" (~2015) and later "Love Local Network"; the only hard numbers are vendor-published (125 new customers/month promise; Winmark 1.6M new customers), none independently audited.

### Cited Findings
- (c. 2015, high confidence on mechanism) "Fivestars Acquisition" featured a business at non-competing Fivestars partner locations on the customer touchscreen, where shoppers could save its rewards; post-visit SMS recapped activity and showcased "nearby rewards from businesses that the customer doesn't yet follow"; app/website offered map-based discovery of nearby businesses. Network then: 30M customers; Fivestars shoppers visit local businesses ~5 times/year; promised "over 125 new customers a month to your customer database"; automatic reminder if a new customer doesn't return in 31 days. Channels: touchscreens, SMS, email, push, app, web. — [Fivestars blog, Acquisition launch](https://blog.fivestars.com/fivestars-acquisition-new-way-reach-automatically-engage-customers-love-local/)
- Post-visit SMS content: recap of in-store activity, current points, rewards saved from the touchscreen, and nearby rewards from businesses the customer doesn't follow; followed businesses are hidden from the discovery map. (Seen via search snippet of Fivestars pages; medium confidence) — [Fivestars blog](https://blog.fivestars.com/fivestars-acquisition-new-way-reach-automatically-engage-customers-love-local/)
- Love Local Network page (undated, pre-/around-acquisition era): 70M customers, 12,000–14,000+ merchants; app "highlights nearby businesses" and attracts customers with an "introductory offer"; claims Fivestars customers are "58% more likely than the general population to favor small businesses" and "25% more likely to spend more money on the same item from a local retailer." No pricing or conversion metrics published there. — [Fivestars Love Local Network](https://fsweb.fivestars.com/products/love-local-network/)
- Case study (vendor-published, SumUp site, undated): Winmark (5 resale brands) has 19M unique customers in Fivestars; "1.6 million new customers acquired through Love Local"; network described as 60M+ consumers; customers receiving Fivestars messages 2x more likely to return within six months vs non-recipients; SMS "95% read rate"; multi-store point sharing. — [SumUp business guide, Winmark case](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/business-guide/enterprise/winmark-grows-with-fivestars-loyalty/)
- Current (2026) SumUp Connect still has a network feature, "Nearby": "Your business name and location will appear at checkout on the customer facing screen at nearby businesses also using the Fivestars by SumUp loyalty system" (non-competing, complementary businesses). — [SumUp Connect automated marketing](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/loyalty-program/automated-marketing/)

### Inferences
- The network's acquisition engine was the physical checkout screen of other merchants plus the transactional post-visit SMS — i.e., discovery piggybacked on messages/screens the consumer was already going to see, rather than on separate ad messages.
- "New customers" figures count sign-ups to a merchant's database via network surfaces, not proven incremental paying visits; Winmark's 1.6M over an unstated period is a vendor case study.

### Gaps
- No independent data on the share of a merchant's customers that came from the network, or on incrementality.
- Whether Acquisition/Love Local was a separately priced add-on (and its price or CPA) — not found on accessible pages.

## 2. Pricing: base vs marketing plans, prices, per-message cost, contracts; why expensive

### Takeaway
Historically Fivestars was a flat monthly subscription (tablet/touchscreen included), commonly reported from ~$299/month, sold by sales reps on 12-month auto-renewing contracts, with marketing capacity (promotions/month, AutoPilot campaigns) gated by tier rather than charged per SMS. Today (2026) SumUp Connect lists public prices: $269/$349 standalone, $199/$289 bundled with SumUp POS, and says "no complicated contracts."

### Cited Findings
- Historical tiers: "Essentials" and "Pro"; Essentials allowed 1 promotion/month and 8 AutoPilot campaigns, included a complimentary customer touchscreen; Fivestars Pay was an add-on at undisclosed cost; pricing "starts at $299 per month", quote-based. (Aggregator; medium-low confidence, undated) — [Saufter breakdown](https://saufter.io/fivestars-pricing-features-reviews-complete-breakdown/) (403 on fetch; seen via search snippet); [ITQlick pricing](https://www.itqlick.com/fivestars/pricing)
- Merchant complaints: told month-to-month but found a 1-year contract; billed after cancelling and threatened with collections; one merchant cites a 10-month remaining contract at $300/month. (Aggregated reviews; medium confidence on pattern, individual cases unverified) — [ITQlick](https://www.itqlick.com/fivestars/pricing); [BBB profile, rated D-](https://www.bbb.org/us/ca/san-francisco/profile/internet-marketing-services/fivestars-loyalty-inc-1116-464125/customer-reviews)
- Rolling 12-month contract that must be cancelled by phone/email before renewal; a merchant reports $2,400 pulled from their account and refund conditioned on signing a new annual contract. (Review snippets; medium-low confidence) — [Capterra UK reviews](https://www.capterra.co.uk/reviews/131516/fivestars); [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/www.fivestars.com)
- Current SumUp Connect pricing page (fetched 2026-09-29): standalone Connect Plus $269/mo (customer directory, AutoPilot, "2 standard promos per month"); Connect Pro $349/mo ("UNLIMITED AI-enhanced messaging", review boost, text-to-join). Bundled POS + Connect Plus $199/mo ("Weekly SMS and email promotions", installation and training); POS + Connect Pro $289/mo ("Unlimited SMS and email promotions"). Money-back guarantee; "No hidden fees... No complicated contracts." No per-message fee listed. — [SumUp Connect pricing](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/loyalty-program/pricing/)
- CONFLICT: search-engine snippets/third-party reviews describe an earlier SumUp Connect lineup of Lite $99, Plus $199, Pro $289/month (Plus = AutoPilot + 2 promos/month). Likely an earlier version of the same page or a reviewer's reading of the bundled prices; not reconciled. — [tech.co SumUp POS review](https://tech.co/pos-system/sumup-pos-review) (snippet only)
- Low-quality source (2026 blog): SumUp rebranded the merchant product "SumUp Connect" around April 2024 and moved month-to-month plans to annual terms during 2024. UNVERIFIED. — [Food AI Daily](https://foodaidaily.com/blog/2026-08-06-best-fivestars-alternatives-independent-restaurants-2026/)
- Justification Fivestars itself offered: "10X more effective than traditional marketing"; customers return "twice as often"; 70M members, "1 in 6 Americans". — [Fivestars homepage](https://fsweb.fivestars.com/)

### Inferences
- The price was justified as bundled hardware (touchscreen), done-for-you install/training, unlimited or high-volume SMS included (no per-message metering visible), automation, and access to the network — i.e., priced like a marketing agency retainer, not like a software seat. The complaints concentrate on contract lock-in, not on the monthly amount per se.
- Marketing monetization was tiering by number of promotions/campaigns per month (1 vs 2 vs weekly vs unlimited), which also doubles as a volume control on consumer messaging.

### Gaps
- No primary (fivestars.com) historical price sheet found; archive.org unreachable here.
- No per-SMS cost for merchants found in any era.

## 3. Frequency coordination across merchants (caps, throttling, quiet hours)

### Takeaway
No documented consumer-level, cross-merchant SMS cap was found. What exists: per-merchant volume limits via plan tiers, a 2014 guideline of 1–3 messages/month per merchant, opt-out monitoring, and a current SumUp claim that "the system prevents message overlap" (scope unspecified — appears to be within one merchant's AutoPilot vs manual campaigns).

### Cited Findings
- (c. 2014) Fivestars guidance: messaging "1-3 times a month is a good start"; monitor opt-out rates (~1% of members opted out) and reduce frequency if they rise. — [Fivestars blog, promotions stats](https://blog.fivestars.com/fivestars-promotions-stats-metrics/)
- Plan limits: Essentials 1 promotion/month, 8 AutoPilot campaigns (historical, aggregator); Connect Plus 2 standard promos/month (2026). — [ITQlick](https://www.itqlick.com/fivestars/pricing); [SumUp pricing](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/loyalty-program/pricing/)
- 2026 SumUp Connect FAQ: "The system prevents message overlap to avoid overwhelming customers"; manual campaigns work alongside AutoPilot without duplication; "Every message includes easy opt-out options, and we're fully TCPA compliant"; customers can opt out of messages while staying in the loyalty program. No frequency FAQ. — [SumUp Connect automated marketing](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/loyalty-program/automated-marketing/)
- Network promotion of other merchants was folded into the merchant's own post-visit recap SMS rather than sent as separate messages. — [Fivestars blog, Acquisition](https://blog.fivestars.com/fivestars-acquisition-new-way-reach-automatically-engage-customers-love-local/)

### Inferences
- Because each consumer "followed" each merchant separately and each merchant sent its own promos, a consumer in many programs could receive messages from many merchants; the design mitigations appear to be (a) tier caps per merchant, (b) bundling cross-merchant discovery into the transactional recap instead of extra sends, and (c) opt-out per merchant.

### Gaps
- No evidence found of a global per-consumer cap, quiet hours, or cross-merchant throttling rule. Whether "prevents message overlap" applies across merchants is not stated — flag as unverified.

## 4. Channels and why SMS

### Takeaway
SMS was the primary channel (phone number = identity at the touchscreen, no app required), with app push and email as secondary; Fivestars' own 2014 data showed app push performed better per recipient, but SMS reached everyone.

### Cited Findings
- Promotions delivered via text, app notification, email; app notification users "63% more likely to claim" than SMS recipients; claim benchmark 5–10%, visit/redemption target ≥10%. (c. 2014) — [Fivestars blog, promotions stats](https://blog.fivestars.com/fivestars-promotions-stats-metrics/)
- AutoPilot (launched July 2014, covered by TechCrunch) automated coupons based on past purchase behavior via "SMS, location-based, geo-targeted push notifications, and email". — [Fivestars blog, AutoPilot/TechCrunch](https://blog.fivestars.com/what-is-autopilot/)
- SMS "95% read rate" (vendor claim). — [SumUp Winmark case](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/business-guide/enterprise/winmark-grows-with-fivestars-loyalty/)
- 2026 channels: SMS, email, push. — [SumUp Connect](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/loyalty-program/automated-marketing/)

### Inferences
- SMS was chosen because enrollment was phone-number-at-checkout with no download; the app was a minority channel even if higher-converting.

### Gaps
- Share of members with the app vs SMS-only not found.

## 5. SumUp acquisition and current state

### Takeaway
SumUp bought Fivestars for $317M (cash + stock), announced 2021-10-13/14, closed 2021-10-28. The consumer network and "Fivestars by SumUp" brand survive on the consumer side; the merchant product is now "SumUp Connect", sold standalone or bundled with SumUp POS, and still includes the network "Nearby" feature.

### Cited Findings
- $317M cash and stock; Fivestars valued $285M at Oct 2020 Series D ($52.5M); 12,000 merchants, 70M consumers, 100M transactions/yr, $3B annual sales; brand kept initially; plan to integrate into SumUp, cross-sell card readers, possibly expand loyalty to Europe/LatAm; CEO Victor Ho and SF team stayed. — [TechCrunch 2021-10-13](https://techcrunch.com/2021/10/13/european-point-of-sale-provider-sumup-acquires-customer-loyalty-startup-fivestars-for-317m/)
- Completion on 2021-10-28. — [SumUp press](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/press/fivestarsacquisition/)
- fivestars.com product pages now 301-redirect to sumup.com/en-us/loyalty-program/ ("SumUp Connect"). — observed redirect from [fivestars.com/products/promotions](https://www.fivestars.com/products/promotions/) to [SumUp Connect](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/loyalty-program/automated-marketing/)
- 2026 SumUp Connect features: AutoPilot (birthdays, post-visit, re-engagement), AI message writing, review boost, "Nearby" cross-promotion on other merchants' checkout screens "using the Fivestars by SumUp loyalty system", dashboard. — [SumUp Connect](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/loyalty-program/automated-marketing/)
- Consumer Fivestars app still listed on Google Play (details not retrievable). — [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fivestars.FiveStarsConsumer&hl=en_US)

### Inferences
- The network was kept (not shut down) but de-emphasized: marketing now sells "Nearby" as one feature rather than "Love Local / 70M members" as the headline; loyalty became an attach product to SumUp POS (bundled price cheaper than standalone).

### Gaps
- No evidence found of European/LatAm rollout of the loyalty product.
- Current network size (members/merchants) not published by SumUp.
- Exact rebrand date (claimed April 2024) unverified.

## 6. Published results and source quality

### Takeaway
All outcome claims are vendor-published marketing (2x return visits, 10x more effective, +70% visits, 125 new customers/month, Winmark 1.6M); no third-party or academic evaluation found.

### Cited Findings
- "Twice as often" return; "10X more effective than traditional marketing". — [Fivestars homepage](https://fsweb.fivestars.com/)
- Promotions "can increase in-store visits and drive future full price purchases by 70 percent" (c. 2014). — [Fivestars blog](https://blog.fivestars.com/fivestars-promotions-stats-metrics/)
- Winmark: message recipients 2x more likely to return in 6 months vs non-recipients (selection bias: not a controlled comparison). — [SumUp Winmark case](https://www.sumup.com/en-us/business-guide/enterprise/winmark-grows-with-fivestars-loyalty/)
- Reputation signal: BBB rating D-; recurring review theme is contract lock-in and billing. — [BBB](https://www.bbb.org/us/ca/san-francisco/profile/internet-marketing-services/fivestars-loyalty-inc-1116-464125/customer-reviews); [Capterra UK](https://www.capterra.co.uk/reviews/131516/fivestars)

### Inferences
- The "2x" comparisons compare messaged (enrolled, engaged) customers vs non-messaged ones — likely confounded; treat as upper bound marketing claims.

### Gaps
- No independent ROI or incrementality study found.
