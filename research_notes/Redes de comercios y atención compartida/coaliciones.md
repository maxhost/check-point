# Coalition loyalty programs (Nectar, Payback, Air Miles, Plenti, LatAm) — monetization, partner value, communications governance, failures, local/SMB schemes

Research date: 2026-09-29. Confidence tags: [H] high (primary source or multiple independent sources), [M] medium (single reputable secondary source), [L] low / aggregator / unverified. Budget: ~17 tool calls; several primary documents (10-Ks, annual reports) were found but not fully read — noted in Gaps.

## 1. Revenue model: who pays whom, per-point fees, breakage, data/media

### Takeaway
The classic coalition sells points to partners ("sponsors") at a per-point fee that bundles the redemption cost, the operator's service margin and a brand/marketing component; profit comes from the spread plus breakage (points never redeemed). Mature coalitions (Nectar, Payback) have shifted value toward data, analytics and retail-media sold to brands — Nectar360 is now primarily a Sainsbury's retail-media business.

### Cited Findings
- Air Miles (Canada): sponsors "pay LoyaltyOne a fee per AIR MILES reward mile issued, in return for which LoyaltyOne provides all marketing, customer service, rewards and redemption management." [H] — [Alliance Data 10-K FY2019 (via search excerpt)](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1101215/000110121520000049/ads-20191231x10k.htm)
- Air Miles revenue recognition: consideration per mile issued is split into three performance obligations — redemption, service, and brand — and redemption revenue depends on management's breakage estimate (miles expected never to be redeemed), based on historical collector behaviour and engagement. [H] — [Alliance Data 10-K FY2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1101215/000110121520000049/ads-20191231x10k.htm); [Loyalty Ventures 10-K FY2021](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1870997/000155837022002348/lylt-20211231x10k.htm)
- Breakage described as "a critical revenue driver… miles issued but never redeemed, representing pure profit." [M] — [The Wise Marketer, Special Report: AirMiles](https://thewisemarketer.com/special-report-airmiles-the-end-of-an-era-in-loyalty-marketing/)
- Generic per-point fee illustration: partners "typically pay the coalition a fee per point (e.g., $0.01 per point), which funds the redemption liability and coalition operations." [L — vendor/aggregator content, illustrative not a published price] — search summary of coalition guides e.g. [wployalty.net](https://wployalty.net/coalition-loyalty-programs/)
- Plenti (US): redemption value was $10 per 1,000 points (i.e., 1 cent/point), cited as "one of the main reasons many retailers decided to leave." [M — secondary] — [Coleman Insights](https://colemaninsights.com/coleman-insights-blog/why-the-plenti-loyalty-program-failed); see also [Forbes, 2018-05-01](https://www.forbes.com/sites/warrenshoulberg/2018/05/01/no-good-and-plenti-as-the-customer-loyalty-program-winds-down/)
- Nectar360 (Sainsbury's) — the entity that "owns and operates Nectar, the UK's largest coalition loyalty scheme" and manages retail media for Sainsbury's and Argos. Revenue £122.4m in FY2022/23 (Mar 2022–Mar 2023), up ~10% from £111m. Sainsbury's targets £100m additional profit through Nectar360 over three years (by 2027), with £39m delivered in year one. [M — Statista + trade press] — [Statista](https://www.statista.com/statistics/1455250/nectar-360-services-revenue/); [Nectar360](https://www.nectar360.co.uk/); [WARC](https://www.warc.com/content/feed/digital-nectar-fuels-sainsburys-retail-media-play/10587)
- Nectar coalition still sells to outside brands: 500+ brand partners claimed; recent additions Marriott Bonvoy, Merlin Entertainments, Uber/Uber Eats. [M] — [search summary of Nectar360 releases](https://www.nectar360.co.uk/)
- Payback (Germany) sells data-driven marketing to partners: "insights from a huge repository of customer data across all partners," campaign tailoring and measurement. Payback also runs a research panel (170,000+ panelists in Germany; 8,000 in Austria) — i.e., a market-research product on top of the coalition. [H for panel figures, primary] — [Payback Group — Performance](https://www.payback.group/en/group/payback/performance)
- Livelo (Brazil, Bradesco + Banco do Brasil, launched 2016 with ~10m customers from merging the two banks' programs): revenue source explicitly identified as retailers buying Livelo points to reward their own customers; positioned as "a CRM tool for these establishments." Initial partners ~15 incl. Walmart, Fast Shop, Cnova, Shell, CVC, TAP, Avianca. [M] — [InfoMoney](https://www.infomoney.com.br/consumo/bradesco-e-bb-lancam-oficialmente-programa-de-fidelidade-com-10-milhoes-de-clientes/); [Consumidor Moderno](https://consumidormoderno.com.br/conheca-empresa-de-fidelidade-que-uniu-bradesco-e-banco-do-brasil/)
- Dotz (Brazil): now describes itself as an "ecosystem" of coalition loyalty + SuperApp + Techfin; Q4 2024 EBITDA +R$11m; Techfin revenue R$86.5m in 2024 (+46%), i.e., growth came from fintech (Dotz Pay, BNPL "Dotz Parcela") rather than the coalition itself. [M — press based on company results] — [Estado de Minas, 2025-03](https://www.em.com.br/mundo-corporativo/2025/03/7096021-dotz-evolui-rentabilidade-com-resultado-positivo.html); primary: [Dotz 4T23 results PDF](https://api.mziq.com/mzfilemanager/v2/d/93e23770-d891-4a7f-92f7-d2d19b6125d2/11cf9832-5b0e-c98b-27ba-27c1f4bc1885?origin=1)
- Puntos Colombia: 50/50 JV between Grupo Éxito and Bancolombia (announced April 2017), a single points pool fed by allied merchants; launched with ~13m customers and a first-year billing target of COP 600bn; 17 initial allies (Frisby, Esso, Mobil, Cine Colombia, fashion brands, etc.). [M] — [Grupo Éxito press](https://www.grupoexito.com.co/es/noticias-grupo-exito/nace-puntos-colombia-la-alianza-entre-el-grupo-exito-y-bancolombia-que-beneficiara-inicialmente-mas-de-10-millones-de-clientes); [Portafolio](https://www.portafolio.co/negocios/empieza-a-operar-en-forma-puntos-colombia-519727)

### Inferences
- The per-point fee is effectively a prepayment for a future reward plus a margin; the operator's profitability is highly sensitive to breakage, so any operator move that reduces breakage risk for members (or increases it, like expiry) becomes a political flashpoint (see Air Miles 2016).
- The durable coalitions ended up owned by (or anchored on) the largest issuer — a grocer (Sainsbury's/Nectar), a grocer+bank JV (Puntos Colombia), banks (Livelo), or a bank buying it out of insolvency (BMO/Air Miles). The data/media business accrues mostly to that owner.
- For a small-merchant network, the analogue of "per-point fee" is a merchant-funded reward; the analogue of the data/media business is selling anonymised audience/visibility to brands or to other members — which only works at scale.

### Gaps
- No published per-point price for Nectar, Payback or Air Miles found (commercial terms are confidential). Did not locate Air Miles' reported breakage percentage in the 10-K within budget.
- Payback Group's own revenue (owned by American Express since 2011 per common knowledge — not verified in this session) not found.
- Club La Nación (Argentina) economics not verified: the "150 comercios / 20%" result found is **Club La Nación of Costa Rica** (nacion.com / elfinancierocr.com), not Argentina — do not conflate. Argentina's Club LA NACION is a subscriber-benefit club tied to a paid newspaper subscription ([club.lanacion.com.ar](https://club.lanacion.com.ar/)); who funds the discount (merchant vs. publisher) was not found.

## 2. Partner value proposition: cross-shopping, new customers, category exclusivity

### Takeaway
Academic evidence shows cross-buying across partners does grow over time and is associated with higher frequency and basket, but promotion responsiveness inside a multi-vendor program can be low and cannibalisation among partners is real; benefits skew to the most popular partners. Category exclusivity (one partner per sector) is the standard design and a core selling point, and it is exactly what breaks when an anchor expands into adjacent categories.

### Cited Findings
- Dorotic, Fok, Verhoef & Bijmolt (Erasmus; "Do vendors benefit from promotions in a multi-vendor loyalty program?", Marketing Letters): studying five main vendors in a multi-vendor program, cardholders showed **low responsiveness to LP-induced promotions** (individual-vendor and joint). [H — peer-reviewed] — [Erasmus repository](https://pure.eur.nl/en/publications/do-vendors-benefit-from-promotions-in-a-multi-vendor-loyalty-prog/); [PDF](https://www.academia.edu/61962056/Do_vendors_benefit_from_promotions_in_a_multi_vendor_loyalty_program)
- "Synergistic and cannibalization effects in a partnership loyalty program": customers can cross-purchase from partners (synergy) but can also switch among partners without forfeiting rewards (cannibalisation); after enrolment customers cross-buy from an increasing number of partners over time; **popular partners attract more new and recurring transactions**. [M — read via abstract/summary only] — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/348892001_Synergistic_and_cannibalization_effects_in_a_partnership_loyalty_program)
- Korea multi-vendor program: cross-buying positively affects subsequent purchase frequency, transaction size and further cross-buying, attenuated for deal-prone customers or those with spending limits. [M — abstract] — [Asian Business & Management (Springer)](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41291-019-00088-w)
- Frontiers in Psychology (2016), "Multi-Vendor Loyalty Programs: Influencing Customer Behavioral Loyalty?" — open-access empirical study on whether MVLP membership changes behavioural loyalty (findings not extracted in detail). [M] — [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4763083/)
- Nectar was founded in September 2002 by four partners in **four different sectors** — Sainsbury's (grocery), Debenhams (department store), BP (fuel), Barclaycard (credit card) — earning at 1,800+ outlets from day one. [H] — [Retail Bulletin, 2002-06-05](https://www.theretailbulletin.com/general-merchandise/four-way-loyalty-scheme-to-launch-05-06-2002/); [Wikipedia: Nectar](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nectar_(loyalty_card))
- Payback reportedly uses an exclusivity clause (competitors of existing partners cannot join), which "increases the commercial value of partnership." [L — from aggregator summary; not confirmed in a Payback primary doc]. Consistent with observed behaviour: when REWE/Penny left at end-2024, **EDEKA and Netto** (competing grocers) joined in 2025 — i.e., one grocery group at a time. [H for the swap] — [Payback Group press: "What will change at PAYBACK in 2025"](https://www.payback.group/en/press/press-releases/detail/what-will-change-at-payback-in-2025-1)
- Aimia's stated reason for selling Nectar (Feb 2018): "the evolution of the Sainsbury's group led to more limited prospects for Nectar to add new **non-competitive** partners" (Sainsbury's had bought Argos), plus loss of Homebase (acquired by Bunnings) and British Gas's exit. [H — Aimia's own release] — [Aimia release](https://www.aimia.com/aimia-sells-nectar-business-to-sainsburys/); [The Drum](https://www.thedrum.com/news/2018/02/01/sainsbury-s-secures-ownership-nectar-aimia-60m)
- Payback performance claims (self-reported): "60% of customers respond to relevant offers"; app users increased purchase activity by 7%; 35% of app users previously didn't use paper coupons and now redeem 50% more than reference groups. [M — primary but marketing claims, no method disclosed] — [Payback Group — Performance](https://www.payback.group/en/group/payback/performance)
- Livelo positioned to partners as a CRM tool (buy points to reward your customers). [M] — [Consumidor Moderno](https://consumidormoderno.com.br/conheca-empresa-de-fidelidade-que-uniu-bradesco-e-banco-do-brasil/)

### Inferences
- Category exclusivity is what makes the coalition sellable to a partner (no competitor gets "your" customer's data or offers), but it caps growth: once the anchor diversifies (Sainsbury's+Argos), the exclusivity grid fills up and third-party recruitment stalls.
- The cross-shopping value exists but is unevenly distributed toward popular/high-frequency partners; smaller partners mostly "rent" the audience of the anchor. A local network should expect the most-visited merchant to be the net receiver of cross-traffic unless offers are deliberately designed to push traffic outward.
- Low responsiveness to program-level promotions (Dorotic et al.) suggests that generic "shared" campaigns underperform; relevance/targeting is where value lies (Payback's own numbers emphasise "relevant offers").

### Gaps
- No published hard numbers (from Nectar or Payback) on % of partner customers acquired from other partners. The academic papers above are the best evidence found.
- No documented industry-wide statistic on how common strict category exclusivity is; evidence is per-program.

## 3. Governance of communications across partners (who decides, frequency caps, ranking)

### Takeaway
In the big coalitions, the **operator owns the member channel** (newsletter, app, statement mailings) and partners buy placement/coupons in it; targeting is by segment/purchase data. No coalition publishes its internal frequency caps or ranking rules; what is visible is operator-controlled cadence (e.g., weekly coupon mail) and operator-orchestrated joint campaigns.

### Cited Findings
- Payback (Germany): 7–10 million active members receive **target-group-specific eCoupons and partner offers every week**; 1.5 billion coupon emails/year; 70 million point-statement mailings/year; open rate >26%, click rate >6.5%; physical mailings 90% opening rate; segmentation on "real purchase data" across offline/online/mobile. [H — primary, self-reported] — [Payback Group — Performance](https://www.payback.group/en/group/payback/performance)
- Payback coupons are distributed "through all PAYBACK channels – in the PAYBACK app, partner apps, points overview, newsletters, and on homepages," and the operator runs joint multi-partner campaigns (e.g., "Welcome to the family", spring promotions) backed by a media mix incl. radio. [H — primary] — [Payback press: largest joint coupon campaign](https://www.payback.group/en/press/press-releases/detail/payback-partners-launch-their-largest-joint-coupon-campaign-welcome-to-the-family); [Payback spring promotion](https://www.payback.group/en/press/press-releases/detail/payback-is-launching-a-major-new-spring-promotion)
- Payback app aggregates ~700 partners (and 680 online partners) in one member interface. [H] — [Payback 2025 release](https://www.payback.group/en/press/press-releases/detail/what-will-change-at-payback-in-2025-1); [Performance](https://www.payback.group/en/group/payback/performance)
- Air Miles: the operator (LoyaltyOne) provided "all marketing" to collectors on sponsors' behalf under the per-mile fee — i.e., the operator, not each sponsor, controlled member communications. [H] — [Alliance Data 10-K FY2019](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1101215/000110121520000049/ads-20191231x10k.htm)
- Data-sharing friction as a governance issue: REWE's exit from Payback was explained partly by less direct control over customer data "since this data was also shared with other Payback partners." [M — trade press] — [Retail Technology Innovation Hub, 2024-12-30](https://retailtechinnovationhub.com/home/2024/12/30/one-to-watch-rewe-takes-loyalty-into-its-own-hands-as-retailer-bids-farewell-to-payback-platform)

### Inferences
- The observable pattern: one central sender, a fixed cadence (weekly digest), and partner content inserted and ranked by the operator using segment relevance. Partners do not message members directly through the coalition channel. This is the model that avoids "many partners spamming the same member."
- Anchor partners get disproportionate share of voice in practice (their offers drive app opens), which is a negotiated, not algorithmic, outcome.

### Gaps
- **No documented frequency-cap rules, slot allocation or offer-ranking algorithms were found** for Nectar, Payback, Air Miles or Plenti. Treat any specific cap number as unverified. Retail-media rate cards (Nectar360) might contain placement rules but were not retrieved.

## 4. Failures and restructurings: Plenti, Air Miles, Nectar, REWE-Payback

### Takeaway
Coalitions fail through anchor-partner exits: when the largest issuers decide the per-point cost exceeds the value (or want their own data), volume collapses, members disengage, and the remaining partners get less. Air Miles additionally self-inflicted damage with a reversed expiry policy. Survivors are those absorbed by their largest partner.

### Inferences (lessons for a local-merchant network)
- Anchor dependence is the main risk: >90% of volume from the top 10 sponsors (Air Miles) means each exit is existential. A local network with one dominant merchant faces the same fragility.
- Big partners leave when (a) cost per point > incremental value, (b) they can build their own program (REWE, Macy's, Sobeys→Scene+), or (c) they want exclusive data. Small merchants cannot build their own easily, so they are structurally better coalition members than big chains.
- Never make changes that devalue or expire member balances abruptly (Air Miles 2016).
- "Unrelated partners" without a shared geography or shopping occasion (Plenti) generate weak cross-shopping; a neighbourhood network has natural co-location that national coalitions lack.
- Coalitions that survive are typically taken in-house by the anchor (Nectar→Sainsbury's, Air Miles→BMO) — i.e., the neutral operator model tends to converge to an anchor-owned model.

- Aeroplan (Air Canada re-acquired it from Aimia in 2019) not researched within budget.
- Plenti member count and financials not found.
- Payback India / Mexico status not researched (Payback India was reportedly scaled down; unverified).

## 5. Local / SMB coalitions (neighbourhood, town-wide, local cards)

### Takeaway
Town-wide schemes exist mostly as (a) paid discount cards for independent shops (Independent Liverpool-style) or (b) shared-identifier "card" platforms where **each merchant runs its own rewards** and points are not pooled — i.e., they deliberately avoid the settlement complexity of a true coalition. Evidence of success is mostly self-reported by councils/vendors; rigorous outcome studies were not found.

### Cited Findings
- Independent Liverpool: consumers buy a card online (£10) giving discounts across participating independent shops/cafés; rolled out to other UK cities after a Liverpool pilot. Model = member pays, merchants give discounts (no points). [M] — [Positive News](https://www.positive.news/economics/loyalty-cards-independent-shops-boost-local-economy/)
- LocalRewards (Ireland, ~2010: Carrigaline Aug 2010, then Macroom, Clonakilty): free town card where each business operates its own reward scheme; points earned at a business redeem only there. Macroom started with 4 businesses; Carrigaline "nearly 3,000 consumers" took and used the card after a September multi-merchant promotion. [M — Brian Woolf retail strategy site reproducing press] — [Brian Woolf](https://www.brianwoolf.com/index?action=bw_study_read&code=h920icfjbi391hbd&t=Irish+town+launches+local+loyalty+scheme)
- UK council schemes (LoyaltyPro platform): Fylde "Shop Local" card (launched Sept 2023, described by the council as "a resounding success"); CH2 "Love Hoole" (Chester) with 2,500+ cardholders and £819,000+ of purchase value processed; again each business runs its own rewards on a shared card, "rather than being a coalition-style programme." ### Inferences
- The recurring local design choice — shared identity/card, **per-merchant** rewards, no pooled currency — sidesteps the hardest coalition problems (inter-merchant settlement, per-point pricing, anchor dominance) while still giving shared discovery. It also gives up the strongest cross-shopping lever (earn here, burn there).
- Local schemes are usually funded/promoted by a council or BID, which substitutes for the "anchor partner" in national coalitions.

### Gaps
- No independent evaluation (academic or audit) of local loyalty-card schemes found; no documented failure post-mortems for local coalitions within budget. Local currencies (e.g., Bristol Pound, closed 2021 — unverified in this session) not researched.
- Argentina-specific local coalitions (e.g., municipal cards, bank-led "Club" programs) not researched beyond Club LA NACION's existence.
